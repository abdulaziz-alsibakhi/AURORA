\section{Domain Validation Strategy}
\label{sec:domain-validation}

AURORA separates four levels of correctness.

First, implementation correctness asks whether the software satisfies its defined
interfaces and algorithms. Second, experimental correctness asks whether the intended
configuration, realization, models, baselines, and evaluation procedure were executed
reproducibly. Third, domain plausibility asks whether the physical interpretation is
supported by appropriate simulator documentation, literature, controlled tests,
cross-variable reasoning, or qualified review. Fourth, real-field validity would
require evidence beyond the scope established merely by successful simulation or
analysis of a real-field dataset.

This separation prevents numerical success from being overstated. Agreement with OPM
supports a claim about the configured numerical reference experiment, not an
unconditional claim about an operating reservoir. Similarly, the existence of a
historical range in Volve does not by itself justify using that range as a physical
safety limit in Egg.

For each important class of data, AURORA records canonical semantics, provenance,
units, temporal and spatial meaning, expected behaviour, relevant relationships,
quality failure modes, and claim boundaries. Data-heavy milestones instantiate a
Data Interpretation Contract that additionally records the task-specific comparator,
expected result, meaningful-change criterion, abnormal/concerning behaviour,
uncertainty, thresholds, response logic, and supporting evidence.

The project distinguishes expected ranges, statistical anomaly thresholds, warning
thresholds, experimental bounds, performance targets, encoded safety constraints,
and strongly justified physical or operational limits. These categories carry
different evidentiary burdens and are not treated as synonyms.

A bounded domain-expert review is planned for important assumptions and
interpretations. Expert review is intended to challenge the project's domain model
and validation rules rather than to approve every simulation run.
